# Principles

> The enduring principles that guide the design, implementation, and evolution of the Functor ecosystem.

These principles are not implementation requirements. They are intended to provide philosophical and architectural guidance for contributors, implementers, and future intelligences participating in the Functor ecosystem.

As technologies, interfaces, and protocols evolve, these principles should remain stable.

---

# 1. Understanding Before Optimization

Functor exists to support understanding.

Performance, scale, automation, and convenience are important, but they should never come at the expense of clarity and comprehension.

A system that executes quickly but obscures meaning is less valuable than a system that makes understanding explicit.

---

# 2. Semantics Before Implementation

Implementation details change.

Semantics endure.

Functor should define the meaning of things before defining how they are stored, transmitted, rendered, or executed.

Whenever possible:

```text
Meaning
    ↓
Interface
    ↓
Implementation
```

should take precedence over:

```text
Implementation
    ↓
Meaning
```

---

# 3. APIs Before Interfaces

Interfaces are projections.

APIs are contracts.

A command-line tool, notebook, desktop application, web application, or intelligent agent should all be capable of interacting with the same underlying system.

Interfaces may evolve independently.

The underlying semantic contracts should remain stable.

---

# 4. Artifacts Over Documents

Documents are a form of artifact.

They are not the only form.

Functor recognizes many types of artifacts:

```text
Specifications
Datasets
Computations
Visualizations
Research
Source Code
Designs
Narratives
```

Artifacts should be treated as first-class entities capable of participating in relationships, validation, and evolution.

---

# 5. Relationships Are First-Class

Meaning emerges from connections.

An isolated artifact contains information.

A network of artifacts contains context.

Functor should make relationships explicit, inspectable, and durable.

Relationships deserve the same level of care as the artifacts they connect.

---

# 6. Validation Over Assumption

Claims should be challengeable.

Understanding should be testable.

Whenever practical, assumptions should be made explicit and validations should be reproducible.

Functor should encourage verification without requiring certainty.

---

# 7. Traceability Matters

Knowledge evolves.

Users should be able to answer:

```text
Where did this come from?
What supports it?
What changed?
Who contributed?
What evidence exists?
```

Traceability is not bureaucracy.

It is memory.

---

# 8. Extensibility Over Specialization

The platform should evolve by extension rather than modification.

A small, durable core is preferable to a large, specialized one.

Functor should provide foundations that allow new capabilities to emerge without requiring reinvention of existing concepts.

---

# 9. Interoperability Over Isolation

Understanding should not be trapped within a single tool, application, protocol, vendor, or ecosystem.

Functor should cooperate with existing technologies whenever possible.

Open integration and collaboration are preferable to fragmentation.

---

# 10. Humans Remain Central

Functor is intended to augment human understanding.

Humans contribute:

```text
Intent
Judgment
Creativity
Experience
Values
```

Technology exists to amplify these capabilities rather than replace them.

The measure of success is improved understanding, not reduced human involvement.

---

# 11. Intelligence Is Collaborative

No individual, team, or intelligence possesses complete understanding.

Knowledge emerges through contribution, revision, disagreement, validation, and synthesis.

Functor should encourage systems that make collaboration easier rather than systems that encourage isolated ownership of knowledge.

---

# 12. Complexity Should Be Optional

Simple things should remain simple.

Advanced capabilities should not burden ordinary workflows.

Users should be able to adopt Functor incrementally and discover additional capabilities as needed.

---

# 13. Stewardship Over Ownership

Knowledge is often inherited from others and entrusted to future participants.

Functor should encourage responsible stewardship of information rather than exclusive ownership of it.

Repositories, artifacts, and relationships should be maintained with future contributors in mind.

---

# 14. Technology Is Temporary

Languages change.

Frameworks change.

Protocols change.

Platforms change.

The ideas represented by artifacts should outlive the technologies used to express them.

Functor should strive for durability over novelty.

---

# 15. Wonder Matters

Not all valuable work is immediately useful.

Exploration, experimentation, and curiosity frequently create understanding that later becomes indispensable.

Functor should leave room for discovery.

Not every artifact needs a predefined outcome.

Not every question needs an immediate answer.

Some of the most important understanding begins with wonder.

---

# Closing Principle

Functor is ultimately an effort to improve the ability of humans and intelligences to create, share, validate, and evolve understanding together.

Every design decision should be evaluated against a simple question:

Does this help intelligent beings understand something more clearly, more accurately, or more completely?

If the answer is yes, the decision is likely aligned with the principles of Functor.
